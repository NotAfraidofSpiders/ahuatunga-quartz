## Summary

With Wethenor breathing down our heroes' necks, the next stretch began with the party waiting, breath held, for Wethenor to pass. Fortunately, the bone whelk's Cygnus scared so the party could slip past had come to investigate the sounds of what might have been a meal. The party's good fortune was not shared by the snails, which quickly drew Wethenor's ire. Wethenor unleashed an unearthly wail which ruptured the head of one snail.

Wethenor began dragging its corpse back up the stairs the party entered through, leaving our heroes with a choice: Find a way to silently move past the remaining snail into the chamber Wethenor had previously occupied, or move uncontested down the path the ghosts directed the party down.

The party was torn, but the smell of ancient parchment emanating from the open door was unmistakable, and our heroes' thirst for knowledge was unsated. Trained in many techniques to move unnoticed, Cygnus cast _Silence_ on the hallway, preventing the sound of Uspia and Crag murdering the whelk from reaching Wethenor.  After that, it was simple to enter the library of the accursed cloister.

Entering the library revealed a holding cell occupied by one of the ghosts which the party encountered roaming the dungeon. The ghost merely stood in the cell, observing the party. One full wall was covered floor to ceiling in bookshelves. A closed door was set into the wall opposite from the door through which the party entered, and an unblocked corridor snaked into the stone next to the entrance.

The library was more barren than any of the party had hoped. Sadly for our heroes, all but one shelf had been cleared of books, but it was obvious from the bowing that these shelves held a great many volumes in their lifetime. The five tomes which remained were all copies of the same text: _Challenging Ancestral Assumptions: A Thesis on Making Contact with The Dead_.

Although the party found 5 copies of the same text, the volumes were not identical. One copy was Wethenor's original manuscript, two were transcribed by a scholar named Odu, and two were transcribed by a scholar named Kaz'era. Upon their discovery, Uspia leapt into translating the original manuscript. From first glance all copies appear identical. The entire time Uspia was reading, the ghost behind the cell bars watched for her reaction intently.

While Uspia was reading, Crag, who is famously not a man of many words, directed his attention towards exploring beyond the door opposite the party's entrance. Behind it he found a massive pile of bones, which shifted and returned the torch which Wade had dropped into them several stretches prior. Uspia paused her reading to retrieve the torch with _mage hand_. The whole party was startled when 3 amalgamations emerged from the pile, thrashing about as though they were drowning in the bones, before sinking back into the mass.

Cygnus began by keeping watch while Uspia read, but quickly became distracted by a heavily damaged tapestry of the night sky. He documented it as best as he could, but the poor condition of the cultural artifact made it difficult to determine which night sky it depicted.

It took Uspia a full stretch, but she was able to make a preliminary translation of Wethenor's Manuscript. While many parts of it make little sense to a speaker of the common tongue, the document was clearly a record of magical research into the nature of death. The manuscript documented the observation process and magical observations as iwi were slaughtered. The deaths were documented chronologically; one-hundred at first, then two-hundred. More untranslated technical language separated the next death records, which detail the deaths of the next several hundred murders. The manuscript continues this pattern until at its conclusion more than seven-thousand iwi (just under half the current population of The Island) had lost their lives to Wethenor's research.

In the time spent translating, several details stood out about Wethenor's work:

- This is the first of many volumes written about the nature of death and magics relating to it
- There are other "facilities" like The Accursed Cloister on the island, although their purposes and locations are currently unknown
- Wethenor achieved his goal of creating a spell which allows mortals to speak with the dead. A scroll detailing the process was fabricated in the cloister before it was sealed.

The revelations revealed by Wethenor's manuscript weighed heavily on our heroes. If it were not obvious to them before, it became readily apparent that nothing good came out of this place. Uspia made contact with the ghost which had been watching the party. Broken communication revealed the ghost to be Odu, one of the scribes of Wethenor's work. Mimicking the postulation of an ancient mural depicting an iwi returning to the elements, Uspia showed her sympathies to Odu. Seemingly satisfied, Odu broke the lock of the cell and disappeared, allowing the party to enter unhindered.

Within the bars of the cell, the party found a single iwi skeleton with a broken neck. All of the body's belongings, save for its hipoki, were lost to time. However, the hipoki was unmistakably Wethenor's. It was not the hipoki his spectre nor statues were wearing. Rather, it was the same form, carved with meaningless and confusing channels and runes. Cygnus was the first of the party to touch the mask, which presented him a vision so vile and repugnant as to compel him to expel his lunch. However mere moments later he could not recall what had disturbed him so thoroughly.

Uspia identified the mask as a [[Hipoki Dagup|Great Hipoki Dagup]], with the Ancient Ahuatungan phrase, "Unknown, not forgotten" carved into the inside of it, just above the forehead. Uspia's motivation unknown to the rest of the party, she also pocketed Wethenor's skull.
