# Tracking Time During a Crawl

## When does the crawl start?

A dungeon crawl, or more simply a "crawl" starts when the first activity which necessitates tracking time begins. This can be as simple as lighting a [[Adventuring Gear#Torch|torch]] or casting a [[Spellcasting Rules#Rituals|ritual spell]], or as nuanced as entering a "hostile" environment (if you're wondering what is or isn't a hostile environment, you found the nuance). Typically, a crawl begins as players decide what to do during the first stretch.

## A Stretch

While crawling, time is measured in 10-minute intervals called stretches. One stretch is enough time for a party of adventurers to cautiously explore a room and mark down directions and for each member to perform a crawling activity.

### Crawling Activities

Several crawling activities are provided below, however any course of action which takes 10 minutes or fewer is a valid crawling activity.

#### Casting a Spell

_**Prerequisite:** You pick a spell you can cast as a part of this Activity._

You spend the next 10 minutes casting a spell that has a [[Spellcasting Rules#Casting Time|casting time]] of 10 minutes, or a ritual spell that has a casting time of 11 minutes or less.

_**Longer Casting Times.**_

- You can do this Activity multiple turns in a row to cast spells with a casting time longer than 10 minutes.
  _**Rolling Initiative**_
- If you roll [[Combat Rules#Initiative|initiative]], while engaged in this Activity, you can continue to cast the spell in combat by maintaining [[Spellcasting Rules#Concentration|concentration]] on the spell and using your [[Combat Rules#Your Turn|action]] each turn to continue casting the spell.

#### Helping Out

_**Prerequisite:** You have a [[Ability Scores#Skills|skill]] or [[Tools|tool]] [[Ability Scores#Proficiency Bonus|proficiency]] relevant to the check being made._

Chose a creature within your reach. You grant that creature [[Ability Scores#Advantage and Disadvantage|advantage]] on ability checks made during these 10 minutes.

#### Interacting

You interact with an object or a part of the environment for the next 10 minutes. This includes, but is not limited to:

- Reading notes, tablets, or inscriptions
- Moving rubble or clearing a path
- Seeking understanding of an esoteric study, container, or complicated device

#### Keeping Watch

_**Prerequisite:** You're not [[Conditions#Blinded|blinded]] or [[Conditions#Deafened|deafened]], and you can see your surroundings clearly._

You stand watch, preparing for any eventuality.

_**Alert the Party.**_

- Your party cannot be [[Combat Rules#Surprise|surprised]] by hostile creatures you can see.
  _**Prepared Motion.**_
- If you roll [[Combat Rules#Initiative|initiative]], you can use a reaction before your first turn to take one of the following actions:
  - [[Combat Rules#Attack|Attack]] (as if making a [[Combat Rules#Ready|readied attack]])
  - [[Combat Rules#Dash|Dash]]
  - [[Combat Rules#Dodge|Dodge]]
  - [[Combat Rules#Hide|Hide]]
  - [[Combat Rules#Use an Object|Use an Object]]
  - [[Combat Rules#Cast a Spell|Cast a Spell]] with a casting time of 1 action.

#### Looting Everything

_**Prerequisite:** you can see and hear clearly._

You spend 10 minutes to collect any [[Adventuring Gear|adventuring gear]] and [[Wealth Rules#Selling Treasure|valuables]] that have already been discovered or are easy to find (determined by your [[Ability Scores#Passive Checks|passive investigation score]])

#### Move Quietly

_**Prerequisite:** [[Ability Scores#Proficiency Bonus|proficiency]] in [[Ability Scores#Dexterity Checks|stealth]]_

For the next 10 minutes, as long as you don't obviously reveal yourself a creature only notices you if it succeeds on a [[Ability Scores#Wisdom Checks|Wisdom (Perception) Check]] to find you. The DC to notice you is 8 + [[Ability Scores#Ability Scores and Modifiers Table|your Dex mod]] + [[Ability Scores#Proficiency Bonus|your proficiency bonus for stealth]].

_**DM Ruling.**_

- D\&D 5e unfortunately has incredibly vague [[Ability Scores#Hiding|hiding rules]]. The regular use of passive perception makes sense for creatures which are idle or are not occupied, however these creatures are few and far between in a dungeon. While crawling, it is assumed that all creatures in a dungeon are occupied enough that their passive perception isn't applicable.

#### Search Area

_**Prerequisite:** you can see and hear clearly_

You spend 10 minutes searching a small room, or an area no larger than 50 feet in any dimension.

_**Ability Check.**_

- Make a [[Ability Scores#Wisdom Checks|Wisdom (Perception)]] or a [[Ability Scores#Intelligence Checks| Intelligence (Investigation)]] check.
  _Success:_
- You find everything in the searched area hidden by a DC equal to or lower than the result of your check. Commonly hidden items in a dungeon are [[Trap Rules|traps]], secret doors, concealed [[Wealth Rules#Selling Treasure|loot and valuables]], and any other hidden items.

#### Take Your Time

You spend the next 10 minutes doing deliberate and methodical work, gaining the following benifits:

_**Taking 20.**_

- You spend time Treat result of the next [[Ability Scores#Ability Checks|ability check]], [[Ability Scores#Saving Throws|saving throw]], or [[Combat Rules#Attack Rolls|attack roll]] (choose one) you make as a 20. If you would have had advantage, you instead get a +5 to the result.
  _**Replace Failure.**_
- In the past 10 minutes, if you made an ability check and failed, you can instead spend this stretch double checking your work to succeed instead, as long as failing the check doesn't make repeating it impossible.

## Resting in a Dungeon

A [[Resting Rules#Short Rest|short rest]] takes 6 stretches. There are some circumstances in which it may be desirable to risk wandering monsters to stop and take a short rest. Trying to rest in a dungeon is a risk, as there are many reasons the rest may be [[Resting Rules#Interrupted Rests|interrupted]].

# Keeping the Pace of Long Crawls

It's no secret that large dungeons can become repetitive, slow, and worst of all tedious. For particularly large dungeons, the following rules can be applied to speed up the pace of the crawl.

## Marching Order

Determine the marching order of the party while they are in corridors or tunnels. The party should decide who is first, who is last, and then the order of the rest of the party in between.

_**Single-File.**_

- 5-foot-wide passageways are explored by a party with a single-file marching order.
  _**Double-File.**_
- 10-foot-wide or wider passageways are explored by a party with a double-file marching order.

## Visibility

Most dungeons are [[Environment Rules#Darkness|dark]] and unlit places. As such, it'll be important to determine how the party is addressing visibility.

_**Determine Light Sources.**_

- The party should choose which of them are carrying light sources, and what sources they are.
- Make a note of those light sources and how many stretches they last.
  _**Darkvision.**_
- For party members with [[Environment Rules#Darkvision|darkvision]], make a note of which characters can see in the dark and at what range.
- Keep in mind that characters with darkvision make [[Ability Scores#Wisdom Checks|perception checks]] with [[Ability Scores#Advantage and Disadvantage|disadvantage]], which decreases [[Ability Scores#Passive Checks|passive Wisdom (Perception)]] scores by 5.

## Default Initiative

Having a standardized default [[Combat Rules#Initiative|initiative]] can save time for minor combat encounters that may happen during a dungeon crawl.

_**Determining Default Initiative**_

- The party should decide amongst themselves who should go first in minor combats, and if they'd prefer to move clockwise or counterclockwise around the table.
  _**Shuffling the Default**_
- While the party is not in initiative, they may choose to change who acts first and which direction around the table turns are ordered in.
  _**Rolling for Monsters**_
- When a monster rolls initiative against the party, roll a die with at-least as many results as there are players. Assign each player a number, with 1 representing the player acting first, and each subsequent number representing the order of that player in initiative. The monster acts after the turn of the player with the number rolled.

# Scales of Challenge

This section is intended for the GM, but curious players are welcome to take a look. The dangers of dungeoneering  are no secret to experienced adventures, so strategizing effective dungeoneering around these rules is _not_ metagaming.

You can adjust the scale and scope of wandering monsters by playing with three dials, they are:
**_Encounter Likeliness_.**

- You adjust what results on the dice start a random encounter.
  _**Encounter Frequency.**_
- You adjust how often you roll to check for random encounters
  _**Encounter Difficulty.**_
- You adjust how tough an individual encounter can be

## Encounter Likeliness

At the end of a stretch, you can roll a d6 to check if the party is attacked by a wandering monster and consulting the following descriptions:

_**Low Frequency.**_

- On a roll of 6 the DM rolls on the Random Encounter Table.
  _**Medium Frequency.**_
- On a roll of 5 or 6 the GM rolls on the Random Encounter Table.
  _**High Frequency.**_
- On a roll of 4, 5, or 6 the GM rolls on the Random Encounter Table.

## Encounter Frequency

You can decide how frequently to roll for wandering monsters by picking one of the following intervals:

_**Constant.**_

- Roll a d6 at the end of every [[Dungeoneering Rules#A Stretch|stretch]] spent exploring the dungeon.
  _**Moderate.**_
- Roll a d6 at the end of every third [[Dungeoneering Rules#A Stretch|stretch]] spent exploring the dungeon.
  _**Hourly.**_
- Roll a d6 at the end of every sixth [[Dungeoneering Rules#A Stretch|stretch]] spent exploring the dungeon.

## Encounter Difficulty

You can decide how challenging the random encounters the players face will be by selecting one of the following difficulty levels:

_**Low Difficulty.**_

- The encounter is likely only to drain one or two player's resources.
  _**Medium Difficulty.**_
- Player Characters will have to spend resources to avoid suffering casualties in the encounter.
  _**High Difficulty.**_
- Random encounters are tough enough to possibly kill a player character.

# Unconventional Uses

This section is intended for the GM, but curious players are welcome to take a look. When used effectively, [[Dungeoneering Rules#A Stretch|stretches]] can be an excellent way to track time and make it important during adventures that may not fit the conventional definition of a dungeon crawl.

## A Ticking Clock

Adventures with a ticking clock could utilize stretches to eschew wandering monsters and instead have a list of events that happens at select stretches in the adventure structure.

Such adventures include:

- A boat, carriage, or caravan trip less than a day in length
- An immanent natural disaster
- A race against rivals

## Haunted Locations

Adventures set in a haunted locale could use stretches to roll for random hauntings that may or may not lead to a random encounter.

## Heists

Heists frequently involve guard rotations, navigating known dangers and challenges, and reacting quickly to newly perceived threats.
